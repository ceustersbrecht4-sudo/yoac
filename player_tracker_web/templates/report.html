04 Coach report

What the report flags, and the rule behind each flag

Pick a team and whether they were attacking or defending. Each flag comes with the moments it happened, a still with the problem marked in red, and a click takes the video there.

Without marking the pitch, distances are in body lengths: the height of a typical player in that frame.

work on

Holes in the defensive line

Two neighbouring defenders much further apart than they usually stand.

gap > 2.2 x usual spacing, and > usual + 1.5 body lengths
work on

Players out of line (dog-legs)

One defender standing well in front of or behind the rest of the line.

> 2.5 x the usual step out of line, at least 1.5 body lengths
work on

Outnumbered in defence / overlap chances

One side has more players standing in the line than the other.

2 or more extra players in the line
work on

Committing too many to the breakdown

Players who join the ruck aren't standing in the line.

defence: 3+ per ruck on average / attack: 4+
timed

Ruck speed

From the players meeting at the tackle until the group breaks up, for every ruck the camera sees start to end. Scrums are counted too.

quick ball < 3 s / slow ball > 6 s / scrum = 10+ players in one group
going well

The same checks, the other way round

A line that stayed connected, kept its shape or matched the attack's numbers is reported as going well.

These are measurements from one camera: a list of moments to rewatch, not a verdict. Check each one on the video.

Mark the pitch once, get metres

On a wide shot, click at least 4 spots where two pitch lines cross, like the 22 and the 5 m line. The app then follows the camera as it pans and zooms, and the report adds:

Line speed in the first 1.5 s after the ball leaves a ruck: under 1.5 m/s is flagged as passive, 2.5 m/s and more as good.
Mauls, told apart from rucks: a breakdown that moves 3 m or more.

Following the camera runs once per video, a few minutes for a full match.
